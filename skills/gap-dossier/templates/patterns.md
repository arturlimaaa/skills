# <target> patterns — what to build in <budget>

Skeleton for the stage 1 brief. Replace every angle-bracket placeholder. Delete
any section the research genuinely could not fill, and say why rather than
padding it. Every claim carries a URL; mark anything unverified for the current
cycle explicitly.

---

## 1. Hard facts and the calendar

A table of the gates that disqualify an entry before it is judged. One row per
gate, each with a source URL: deadlines with time zones, format and length
limits, eligibility and quota rules, required disclosures, submission mechanics.

Then, as separate short subsections:
- the full decision timeline, end to end
- any rule that is new this cycle, called out as new
- the complete list of automatic rejections
- base rates: a table of volume and selection rate per cycle, with sources

State the effective prior for *this* entrant's specific situation, and show the
arithmetic. It is the number that governs every later decision.

## 2. What gets selected

Archetype table: share of accepted work, the fraction achievable on the stated
budget, typical scale of evidence, and two or three exemplars with URLs.

Follow with whatever the sample supports:
- the real scale distribution, as quantiles rather than an average
- what top-honoured entries have that ordinary accepted ones lack
- rhetorical or structural markers that separate the two

## 3. What gets rejected

Ranked table of complaints. Per row: the complaint, its measured cost in the
gatekeeper's own score, and the counter-move stated as an imperative.

Then the score mechanics: the threshold, how steep the curve is around it, and
how different score patterns at the same mean fare differently. This is where an
entrant learns whether to prevent a low score or chase a high one.

## 4. Rising versus saturated

Table with a verdict, a volume, and a selection rate per segment. Include an n
for every row, because stage 2 joins its calibration to these numbers.

Verdicts: rising and thin, rising and crowded, saturated, dead. Say plainly what
segment selection alone is worth, since it is usually worth more than quality
within a segment.

## 5. What fits the budget

Per archetype: does it fit, why, and its characteristic failure mode. Be honest
about which archetypes are out of reach and say what makes them so.

Include a calibration subsection on any assistance the entrant is counting on,
with measured evidence rather than assumption. Optimism about tooling is the
most common planning error.

## 6. Ten imperative rules

Numbered, imperative, specific to this attempt. Each one actionable today. Order
them by when they must happen, not by importance. Anything with an expiry date
goes first.

## 7. Phased execution plan

A table per phase with a date, an action, and the artifact that proves the action
happened. End each phase with an explicit gate: the condition that must hold to
continue, and what to switch to if it does not.

Close with what happens after submission, and any deadline that is irreversible.
